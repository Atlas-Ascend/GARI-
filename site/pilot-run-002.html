What this report proves

Layer 1

Financial reconstruction

state grant ceiling → county reimbursement eligibility → provider allocation → payment and expenditure records still required
  • Agreement 8078: not-to-exceed $3,836,732.32, structured as reimbursement of authorized expenses incurred from July 1, 2023 through June 30, 2025. Requests were required at least quarterly.
  • Regional plan: the public program design paired $2,500,000 for 80 shelter beds with $1,336,732 for rehousing 33 people. These are program allocations inside the grant structure, not verified cash payments.
  • May 2024 state schedule: CCA total allocation $2,669,581, consisting of $1,654,581 rapid rehousing, $650,000 Columbia Inn shelter, $120,000 street outreach, $100,000 capacity building, $50,000 sanitation services, and $95,000 data collection.
  • Later county FAQ: reports a $1,250,000 Columbia Inn allocation over the 2023–25 biennium, described as $625,000 per year for 58 beds. The relationship between this later summary and the May 2024 $650,000 shelter line requires amendment and payment-ledger reconciliation.

No amounts above are summed as independent spending because several represent the same funding at different legal or reporting stages.

Layer 2

Outcome and monitoring reconstruction

Contracted and planned controls

  • Minimum regional target of 80 additional emergency-shelter beds by June 30, 2025.
  • Regional target to rapidly rehouse 33 people by June 30, 2025.
  • Quarterly milestone language anticipated approximately five CCA rehousings per quarter and sustained Columbia Inn operations.
  • Monthly aggregate HMIS confirmation, monthly inventory updates, quarterly funding requests, and performance review duties appear in the state agreement.

Publicly observed snapshots

  • A January 2024 Columbia Inn report states six units moved to permanent housing situations that month.
  • The same report states 89 persons were served and daily occupancy averaged around 60 people. This is one month, provider-reported, and not a final contract-performance determination.
  • The later county FAQ says providers and the county discussed vacant beds and underutilization, followed by program adjustments intended to improve utilization.
  • The public record contains differing capacity descriptions, including 58 beds in the regional plan and 67 in the May 2024 state subgrantee schedule. This remains a version and definition issue.

Layer 3

Publication finding

The public-source pilot is complete enough to publish a coverage finding, not a misconduct finding.

The available record establishes a substantial, formally governed funding and service structure with reimbursement conditions, reporting duties, allocation categories, capacity goals, and outcome targets. It also leaves material gaps: the system has not yet obtained the county reimbursement requests, payment-ledger entries, Columbia Inn aggregate expenditure reports, monitoring and closeout records, complete monthly utilization series, or final validated housing-exit results.

Therefore the correct published conclusion is:

Public records establish how the program was authorized and how funds were allocated, but they do not yet permit a complete reconciliation from authorized funds through county payment, provider expenditure, and independently verified final outcomes.

No accusation authority

Active records action

A written aggregate-only public-record request was sent to the City of Astoria on July 29, 2026 for FY 2024–25 agreements, payments, ledger entries, funding commitments, and performance records involving CCA or Columbia Inn. County and state reimbursement, payment, monitoring, and closeout records remain active acquisition targets.
